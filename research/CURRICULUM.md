# Objection: curriculum design (MBE, July 2027 sitting)

Written 2026-11-02 by the lead-designer agent from the research in this folder: `format.md`, `questions.md`, `civpro.md`, `conlaw.md`, `contracts.md`, `crim.md`, `evidence.md`, `property.md`, `torts.md`, `hooks.md` + `hooks.jsonl`, `skepsis-learning-science.md`, and Carpe's `research/CURRICULUM.md` (the sibling course this one copies). Every exam fact below points at one of those files. Every rule a writer puts into a unit must be checked against primary law (FRE, FRCP, Title 28, UCC Arts 1-2, the Constitution, Supreme Court opinions on law.cornell.edu, Restatements) before it ships; the research files mark what was and was not verified.

Audience: a stressed US law graduate studying 8-12 weeks for the Multistate Bar Examination (MBE). UI language: English, the terminology of the NCBE outline and the standard bar outlines (majority rule / minority rule, common law vs UCC Article 2, FRE, FRCP, "the best answer", elements, standards of review, levels of scrutiny). Tone: warm, witty, encouraging; never childish, never law-firm-ad cheese. Mascot: **Amicus**, an armadillo in a bow tie (amicus curiae, "friend of the court"; the armadillo is the Texas nod), moods happy / sad / cheer. Brand line: "Objection sustained. Bar passed."

**Controlling-law assumptions the outline states** (each unit says its own on its first card, because the exam does): Civ Pro = FRCP as currently in effect + Title 28 jurisdiction/venue/transfer sections. Con Law = the federal Constitution. Contracts = Official Text of UCC Articles 1 and 2. Evidence = FRE as currently in effect. Torts = survival and wrongful-death actions exist; **joint and several liability and pure comparative fault** unless the question says otherwise. Criminal Law and Real Property name no code: common law and the majority rule, with the stem stating any other rule ("The jurisdiction defines crimes as at common law", "a race-notice statute").

**NextGen status (decides the target).** NextGen UBE is live: first given July 28-29, 2026 in CT, ID, MD, MO, OR, WA, Guam, N. Mariana Is., Palau, USVI. No new jurisdiction joins in Feb 2027. **July 2027 adds 12**: AZ, IA, KY, MN, NE, NM, ND, OK, SD, TN, VT, WY (West Virginia conflicts between two NCBE pages, 2027 vs 2028: never state it). Feb 2028: DE, DC, IL, MT. July 2028: 25 more including CA, NY, TX, FL, PA, NJ, OH, GA, MA, MI, NC, VA. The legacy exam runs through Feb 2028. So **the MBE is the target for both 2027 sittings** (every Feb 2027 candidate outside the first ten; the large majority in July 2027). Seven of NextGen's eight concepts are the MBE subjects under the same law, so every item carries `exam: ["mbe"]` or `["mbe","nextgen"]`, and a NextGen mode (select-two-of-six, Business Associations, 12 Model Rules) is a later track, not v1 (`format.md` §5-6).

**Currency.** FRE as amended Dec 1, 2024 (Rules 107, 613(b), 801(d)(2), 1006 new in 2024; 702, 106, 615 in 2023). **FRE 801(d)(1)(A) is amended effective Dec 1, 2026** (Supreme Court order of April 8, 2026, read from the PDF this session: the new text is simply "(A) is inconsistent with the declarant's testimony", so the "under penalty of perjury at a trial, hearing, other proceeding, or deposition" requirement disappears; Congress could still act before Dec 1, so the Evidence writer re-checks on that date). FRCP amended Dec 1, 2025 (Rules 16, 26, new 16.1). Supreme Court decisions from mid-2024 on are unlikely to be scored before 2028 (NCBE says items take about three years and drafters avoid pending issues): they ship as `recent: true` extension cards, never as the only route to a skill.

## 1. Course map (14 units, path order)

Each unit is one file `app/src/units/uN.js`, written by one agent. A lesson is 6-8 items (rule cards count; each rule card carries 2-5 inline quiz questions, so a lesson is 12-25 graded steps and the engine serves it in two sittings). Subject units get 7-8 lessons; the three skills units get 5-6.

| # | Unit (title in the app) | Outline target | Lessons | Items (approx.) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| u1 | Read the call: how an MBE question works | all (skills) | 6 | ~45 |
| u2 | Civil Procedure I: getting into federal court (jurisdiction, removal, PJ, service, venue, Erie) | CP I, II (~2/3 weight with u3's motions) | 8 | ~60 |
| u3 | Civil Procedure II: running the case (pleadings, Rule 11, joinder, discovery, motions, jury, judgments, preclusion, appeals) | CP III-VII | 8 | ~60 |
| u4 | Constitutional Law I: the structure (justiciability, Congress, President, federalism) | CL I-III (~1/2) | 7 | ~52 |
| u5 | Constitutional Law II: individual rights (state action, due process, equal protection, takings, First Amendment) | CL IV (~1/2) | 8 | ~60 |
| u6 | Contracts and Sales | K I-VI, UCC ~1/4 | 8 | ~64 |
| u7 | Criminal Law: crimes, parties, defenses | CR I-IV (~1/2) | 7 | ~52 |
| u8 | Criminal Procedure: the Constitution and the accused | CR V (~1/2) | 8 | ~60 |
| u9 | Evidence (FRE) | EV I-V | 8 | ~60 |
| u10 | Real Property I: ownership and rights in land (estates, future interests, RAP, cotenancy, landlord-tenant, easements, covenants, zoning, fixtures) | RP I-II (2/5) | 7 | ~52 |
| u11 | Real Property II: deals, mortgages and titles (contracts, mortgages, foreclosure, adverse possession, deeds, recording) | RP III-V (3/5) | 7 | ~52 |
| u12 | Torts | T I-IV, negligence ~1/2 | 8 | ~64 |
| u13 | Objection Latin: the vocabulary of the exam (Latin, law French, terms of art, numbers that get tested) | all (terms) | 6 | ~140 term/number cards |
| u14 | Traps, distractors and exam day | all (traps) | 6 | ~90 trap/spot cards |

**Why this split.** Every subject is 25 questions, so the units are equal in exam value; the doubled subjects are the ones whose NCBE note draws a seam that is worth teaching as two halves (Civ Pro I/III/V vs the rest; Con Law structure vs rights; Crim substantive vs constitutional) and Property, whose five equal categories are the widest list (31 modules in `property.md`). Contracts, Evidence and Torts fit eight dense lessons each.

**Path and unlocking.** As in Carpe: sequential inside a unit, but the **first lesson of every unit is open from day one**, because a bar candidate follows a commercial schedule and wants to drill whatever subject is on this week. The path screen is a **courthouse hallway**: 14 doors (courtrooms), the roundup node at the end of each unit is "Closing argument". Recommended order on the home screen: u1 first (20 minutes), then the subject the user picks in settings ("this week I'm on: Evidence"), u13 and u14 any evening.

**Phases on the countdown** (settings: `examDate`, default 2027-07-28; presets Feb 24 2027 / Jul 28 2027 / Feb 23 2028 / custom): weeks 1-6 **Foundations** (learn units; daily drill teaches), weeks 7-10 **Discovery** (mixed practice, one Mini-MBE a week, mistakes notebook), last 14 days **Closing argument** (review only, every due card pulled forward, Cross-Examination for speed, one full 100-question session). With a 10-week horizon the review cap is exam - 3 days from day 1 of Closing argument.

## 2. Content item kinds (the schema writers fill)

Carpe's Latin kinds (`word sentence form role clause transform fact hook origin`) are retired. Objection keeps `rule` and `trap`, and adds the kinds below. Unit file shape is unchanged:

```js
(function () {
  const unit = {
    id: "u9", title: "Evidence (FRE)", blurb: "Hearsay is a quarter of the subject. Relevancy is a third.",
    subject: "evidence",                       // civpro conlaw contracts crim evidence property torts skills terms traps
    assume: "Federal Rules of Evidence as currently in effect (amended Dec 1, 2024; 801(d)(1)(A) changes Dec 1, 2026).",  // shown on the unit's first card
    exam: ["mbe", "nextgen"],
    lessons: [ { id: "u9l1", title: "Is it hearsay?", items: [ /* items */ ] } ],
  };
  if (typeof OBJECTION_UNITS !== "undefined") OBJECTION_UNITS.push(unit);
  if (typeof module !== "undefined") module.exports = unit;
})();
```

Fields every item has: `id` (unique, `u9-803-6-outsider`), `kind`, `say` (teach-it-in-one-breath line, ≤ 160 chars, plain English, no abbreviations the exam does not use), `status`, `src`, and optionally `cat` (outline code, e.g. `"V.D"`), `exam` (`["mbe"]` when the topic is not in the NextGen scope, else inherited), `recent: true` (decided after mid-2024), `lawVersion` (`"fre-2026-12-01"` on anything whose answer changes with the 801(d)(1)(A) amendment; the engine shows the version note), `assume` (the stem-stated rule: `"race-notice statute"`, `"common law"`, `"MPC"`, `"lien theory"`), `split: true` (majority/minority turns on it; the stem must name the rule), `hook` (a story or mnemonic from `hooks.jsonl`, one line), `transfer: true` (asked cold, never introduced; the lesson must teach the rule it needs).

**`status` (provenance), redefined for law:**
- `book`: the rule was read in the primary text this project trusts: FRE, FRCP, Title 28, UCC Arts 1-2, the Constitution, a Restatement section, a Supreme Court opinion or syllabus, a federal statute on LII. `src` names it (`"FRE 803(6)"`, `"FRCP 12(h)(1)"`, `"28 U.S.C. §1441(b)(2)"`, `"UCC 2-207(2)"`, `"Rest. (2d) Contracts §89"`, `"Crawford v. Washington (2004)"`, `"U.S. Const. art. I §10"`).
- `exam`: NCBE itself states it: an outline note or assumption, the outline heading, NCBE's own MEE analysis. **Never a copied NCBE question**; we cannot use their items.
- `general`: standard black-letter doctrine from reliable secondary sources or legal knowledge, not re-read in primary text this project ([K], [M], [R], [SEC], [MEMORY] in the research files).
- `unsure`: anything the research flags [?], [U], [UNSURE], VERIFY, or a real jurisdictional split the stem does not resolve. Shown as "under review"; the daily drill and mocks never draw `unsure` items.

| kind | Fields | Used by |
|---|---|---|
| `rule` | `title`, `say`, `elements` (ordered list of 2-7 short strings; drives the build-from-tiles game; `ordered: false` when order is irrelevant), `exceptions` (list), `split` (optional `{majority, minority, mpc, ucc, commonLaw}` strings, each one line; drives the majority-or-minority game), `cite` (the rule/statute number or case the exam would expect, e.g. `"FRE 613(b)"`; drives rule-number recall), `numbers` (optional list of `{label, value}`: `{label:"Rule 11 safe harbor", value:"21 days"}`), `ex` (1-3 one-line applications `{facts, result}`), `terms` (vocabulary the card introduces), `quiz` (2-5 inline checks: `{t:"mcq", q, options, a, why}`, `{t:"tf", q, a, why}`, `{t:"bucket", q, buckets, items:[{text, bucket}]}`) | every subject unit |
| `hypo` | the MBE item: `stem` (40-220 words; no proper names: "a man", "the retailer", "State A"; one jurisdiction clause when the rule is split), `call` (the lead-in, phrased as the exam phrases it: "Will the plaintiff prevail?", "Is the testimony admissible?", "Should the court grant the motion?", "Of what crime, if any, is the man guilty?"), `callType` (one of the 14 families in §3), `options` (exactly 4 `{text, why, trap}`: `why` names the reason this option is right or wrong; `trap` on wrong options is the distractor type 1-11 from §3, which also tags the mistakes notebook automatically), `answer` (index 0-3; the engine shuffles), `issue` (the doctrine in ≤ 6 words, e.g. "forum-defendant rule": the guess-first "spot the issue" question uses it with three sibling issues from the unit), `rule` (id of the `rule` card it applies; review prefers an unseen sibling hypo with the same `rule`), `grid: true` when the options form the Yes/No x because 2x2 (the engine can then ask verdict first, reason second), `facts` (optional list of 1-3 words/phrases in the stem that decide the answer, for the tap-the-facts question), `transfer`, `assume`, `trapType` (the main temptation), `src` | every subject unit, u14 |
| `argue` | practice-oriented item: `stem`, `call` ("What is the defendant's best argument?", "Which objection should the prosecutor make?", "What is the plaintiff's best option for challenging the judgment?"), `options` (4 arguments; the key is the one the rule actually supports), `answer`, `why` per option, `rule` | subject units (1-2 per lesson), u1 |
| `steps` | a decision tree the exam expects you to run in order: `title`, `steps` (ordered 3-6 strings), `distractors` (2-4 wrong or out-of-order steps), `applyTo` (optional short facts and the step where the analysis stops, for a `steps`+`mcq` combo) | u3 (Rule 12 waiver, removal), u5 (speech framework), u6 (2-207), u8 (Miranda, search analysis), u9 (hearsay, confrontation), u11 (recording-act priority) |
| `pairs` | match set: `set` (name), `pairs` (5-8 `{l, r}`), `spare` (optional 1-2 extra right-hand tiles), `mode` (`"case-holding"`, `"rule-number"`, `"number"`, `"term"`, `"concept"`, `"overruled"`) | all units; u13 heavily |
| `sort` | yes/no or 2-4 bucket sorting: `q`, `buckets` (2-4 labels), `items` (6-12 `{text, bucket, why}`), `mode` (`"yesno"`, `"category"`, `"split"` for majority/minority/MPC/UCC sorting, `"scrutiny"`, `"hearsay"`) | all units |
| `num` | a tested number or deadline: `ask` ("Days to remove after service of the initial pleading"), `answer` (`"30 days"`), `alt` (accepted spellings: `["30", "thirty days"]`), `src`; served as typed answer, 3-option mcq, pair, and Cross-Examination | u2, u3, u6, u9, u11, u13 |
| `case` | `name`, `year`, `holding` (one line, the rule), `facts` (one line, the story hook), `subject`, `overruledBy` / `overrules` (optional), `recent` | u4, u5, u8, u9, u12 (match pairs, "keeping current" cards), u13 |
| `term` | `term`, `lang` (`latin`, `french`, `english`), `literal`, `meaning` (the legal meaning in one line), `use` (the exam context: "FRE 410 nolo plea"), `hook`, `pairWith` (optional contrast term: ejusdem generis / expressio unius) | u13 (and 1-2 per subject unit) |
| `trap` | `wrong` (the tempting option text), `right`, `stem` (short facts or a reference to a `hypo` id), `why`, `cat` (distractor type 1-11 or UT miss tag: `misread`, `unknown`, `misapplied`, `nuance`, `addedFact`, `sympathy`), `subject` | u14, plus 1-2 per subject unit |

Validator rules (`app/tools/validate.js`, to be rewritten): `hypo.options` has exactly 4 with distinct texts and exactly one with no `trap`; `options[*].why` ≤ 240 chars; `stem` has no capitalised proper names other than "State A/B", statute names and case names; `rule.elements` 2-7 entries; every `hypo.rule` resolves to a `rule` id in the same unit (or a listed `dep` unit); `status: "exam"` items must cite an NCBE document in `src`; `unsure` items are allowed but counted and reported; `say` ≤ 160 chars; `cite` on a `rule` must match `/^(FRE|FRCP|FRAP|UCC|28 U\.S\.C\.|U\.S\. Const\.|Rest\.|MPC|[A-Z][\w.' ]+ v\. [\w.' ]+)/`.

## 3. Question types the engine must support

Carpe's renderers are `intro mcq tf build match slots bucket find steps transform spot type swipe`. Objection keeps `intro mcq tf build match bucket find steps spot type swipe`, drops `slots` and `transform` (Latin-only), and adds the ones marked NEW. Every type returns `{type, itemId, label, prompt, ..., answer, explain}` so `app.js` renders it and `engine.grade` scores it.

| # | Type | Carpe trick kept | New for bar prep | Built from |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `issue` NEW (mcq variant) | **guess-first opener** ("Spot the issue", no penalty) | a 1-3 sentence stem, four doctrine names; the learner names the issue before any rule is shown | `hypo.issue` + 3 sibling issues from the unit |
| 2 | `hypo` NEW | multiple choice, 4 options, "the best answer" | the real MBE shape: fact pattern + lead-in + 4 options in the "Yes, because / No, because" or "unless" form; 110-second timer shown (1.8 min pace), never enforced in lessons; after answering, every option's `why` is shown and the chosen wrong option's `trap` type is named | `hypo` |
| 3 | `grid` NEW | two-step mcq | verdict first (Yes/No, Admit/Exclude, Grant/Deny), then the reason among the two options that share that verdict: teaches that the reason, not the result, decides | `hypo` with `grid: true` |
| 4 | `transfer` | **apply the rule to an unseen fact pattern** | a `hypo` whose `rule` was taught but whose facts are new; always in the practice phase, never introduced; review prefers an unseen sibling | `hypo` with `transfer: true` |
| 5 | `argue` NEW | mcq | practice-oriented lead-ins: strongest argument, best objection, best response, best option; the key is the argument the rule supports on these facts | `argue` |
| 6 | `build` | **assemble from tiles** | the elements of a claim, defense or test in order (standing; adverse possession; 803(6) foundation; Dole; the foreclosure waterfall), with 1-3 distractor tiles; `ordered: false` sets are checked as sets | `rule.elements` |
| 7 | `steps` | ordered decision chain (Carpe's clause builder) | a decision tree in order (hearsay: statement? out of court? for truth? 801(d)? 803? 804? Confrontation?) | `steps` |
| 8 | `match` | pairs, with spare | case ↔ holding, rule number ↔ rule, number ↔ deadline, term ↔ meaning, overruled ↔ overruling case | `pairs`, `case`, `num`, `term` |
| 9 | `bucket` / `tf` | **yes/no sorting**, 2-4 buckets, fast | hearsay or not; state action or not; which scrutiny; waived or not; testimonial or not; CL or UCC | `sort`, `rule.quiz` |
| 10 | `stance` NEW (bucket variant) | sorting | **majority or minority**: a one-line rule statement, buckets "Majority", "Minority", "MPC" (crim) / "Common law", "UCC Article 2" (contracts) / "Traditional", "Restatement (Third)" (torts, property); the explanation always says what the stem would have to say to make the minority rule apply | `rule.split`, `sort` with `mode: "split"` |
| 11 | `cite` NEW (type/mcq) | typed answer | **rule-number recall**: "Which rule lets a summary of voluminous records in?" → `1006`; "Which Rule 12(b) defenses are waived if omitted?" → `2, 3, 4, 5`; typed with a "show me options" fallback (no penalty first time), 3-option mcq in Cross-Examination | `rule.cite`, `num` |
| 12 | `type` | typed answer with normaliser | numbers and deadlines (`$75,000`, `21 days`, `14 days`, `10 depositions`); normaliser strips `$`, commas, "days", case | `num` |
| 13 | `find` | tap words in the text | tap the fact in the stem that fails the element (pattern 16), or the fact an option assumes that the stem never says (pattern 18) | `hypo.facts` |
| 14 | `spot` | tf + follow-up mcq (**spot the trap**) | an option with its reasoning is shown as a student's answer: is it the key or a distractor? then name the distractor type (right result/wrong reason, wrong body of law, assumed fact, absolute words, true-but-irrelevant, stale rule...) | `trap`, `hypo.options` |
| 15 | `swipe` | self-graded flashcard, 3 s | fast rule recall for Cross-Examination and the 5-minute day: "Words alone are adequate provocation" → No | `rule.quiz` tf items, `num`, `term` |
| 16 | `intro` | teach card | rule card (title, one-breath line, elements, exceptions, majority/minority, cite, one example, hook); case card; term card; the unit's assumption card | all |

**The eleven distractor types** (`hypo.options[*].trap`, `trap.cat`; from `questions.md` §5, NCBE's item-writing rules plus the prep consensus): 1 right result, wrong reason · 2 right rule, missing element · 3 assumed fact · 4 wrong body of law or wrong standard (CL vs UCC, wrong scrutiny tier, wrong burden) · 5 misstated rule · 6 true statement that does not answer the call · 7 absolute words (always/never/only) · 8 sympathetic facts · 9 near-twin (less complete rule) · 10 impressive doctrine, wrong fit · 11 stale rule (pre-Dobbs, pre-Gant, Lemon, Humphrey's Executor).

**The fourteen lead-in families** (`hypo.callType`; `questions.md` §8A): `prevail` · `admissible` · `constitutional` · `jurisdiction` (proper removal / SMJ / PJ / venue) · `motion` (how should the court rule) · `crime` (of what crime, if any; includes "No crime") · `reverse` (should the conviction be reversed) · `status` (void / voidable / enforceable) · `title` (who wins the land) · `accurate` (which statement is most accurate) · `instruct` (how should the judge instruct) · `argument` (best argument / option / objection, served as `argue`) · `remedy` (entitled to reject / specific performance / damages amount) · `liable` (liable for the deficiency / to whom).

**Lesson flow** (engine, same as Carpe): (1) guess-first `issue` or cold `hypo` on the lesson's first rule, no penalty; (2) for each `rule`: intro card → its inline quiz; for each `case`/`term`: intro → match or mcq; (3) practice: `hypo` and `transfer` shuffled with `build`, `stance`, `cite`, `find`, `grid` on the lesson's rules; (4) `match` when the sitting has ≥ 4 pairable cards; (5) 1-2 older due items interleaved; (6) missed questions return at the end ("Overruled: try again"). Perfect lesson: confetti, Amicus cheers. Amicus looks sad only in the explanation panel, never blocks progress.

**Cross-Examination** (speed round, "one wrong answer and the witness walks"): 3-option questions, 15 s each, pools `num`, `cite`, `sort` rows served as tf, `pairs` rows, `term`, `rule.quiz` tf; score = questions survived; best score badge; pulls only from learned items (box ≥ 1), else from u1/u13.

## 4. Game features (courtroom flavour, Skepsis/Carpe mechanics)

- **XP = billable minutes.** 10 per lesson, +5 for a perfect lesson, 3 per Cross-Examination witness kept on the stand, 15 per Mini-MBE, 1 per Question of the Day question. The profile shows "billed this week: 4h 20m" (minutes, nothing is ever "lost").
- **Daily goal** tiers: **Coffee break** (10 minutes: one lesson sitting or the Question of the Day), **Billable hour** (20), **All-nighter** (40, named with a wink: the app tells you to sleep in the last week). A **5-minute minimum day** keeps the streak: one Question of the Day or one review set.
- **Streak** ("days on the docket"): weekends never break it; one free freeze ("continuance") a week, +1 every 7 days, max 2. No hearts, no lives, no punishment. Amicus holds a gavel when a continuance was used.
- **Ranks (profile title, by XP and milestones):** 1L → 2L → 3L → Bar Applicant → Associate → Senior Associate → Partner → Judge → Justice.
- **Badges:** First Objection (first lesson), Sustained (7-day streak), Of Record (one lesson in every subject), Rule Book (all `cite` cards of a subject in box ≥ 3), Closing Argument (a unit roundup at 100%), Cross-Examiner (Cross-Examination 15+), Docket Clerk (7 Questions of the Day in a row), Moot Court (first Mini-MBE), Bench Trial (a full 100-question session), Juris Doctor (every unit's roundup done), Bar Passed (a Mini-MBE at 22/25 or better three times), Sine Errore (a perfect Mini-MBE), Amicus Curiae (u13 complete), Objection Sustained (u14 complete).
- **Amicus moods:** happy (default), cheer (perfect / badge / streak milestone), sad (after a miss, with the one-breath fix), robe (Cross-Examination and mocks), sleepy (countdown screen after 23:00, "the exam is in the morning; so is the law").
- **Countdown chip:** "268 days to Wednesday, July 28, 2027" with the three phases (Foundations, Discovery, Closing argument) drawn as a courthouse hallway. The default date is stored in one constant `Engine.EXAM_DATE = "2027-07-28"` with `EXAM_TIME = "09:00"`, flagged "derived from NCBE's last-Wednesday rule; confirm with your board", and overridable in settings with presets **Feb 24, 2027 (confirmed)**, **Jul 28, 2027 (derived)**, **Feb 23, 2028 (last legacy MBE, derived)** and a custom date. Settings also hold **"I sit: MBE / NextGen"** (NextGen shows a banner: "Objection drills the seven MBE subjects that NextGen also tests; it does not cover Business Associations, integrated sets or performance tasks yet") and **"This week I'm on: [subject]"** (drives the suggested lesson and the swipe pool).

## 6. Accuracy and provenance rules (for every writer)

1. **Never invent a rule, element, number, holding or majority position.** Sources in order of authority: the primary text (LII: FRE, FRCP, Title 28, UCC; supremecourt.gov / LII opinions and syllabi; Restatement sections where quoted in the research), then NCBE's own documents (outline notes and assumptions, MEE analyses), then the research files' [V]/[READ]/[P] claims, then their [K]/[M]/[R]/[SEC] claims (`status: "general"`), then anything flagged [?]/[U]/[UNSURE]/VERIFY (`status: "unsure"`, shipped only as "under review").
2. **State the rule set in the stem whenever it is split**, because the MBE does: "The jurisdiction defines crimes as at common law", "The jurisdiction has a race-notice recording statute", "Assume a lien-theory jurisdiction", "The jurisdiction applies pure comparative fault" (the Torts default; say it only when the point of the item is the default). Never make an item whose answer depends on an unstated minority rule.
3. **UCC or common law first.** Every Contracts hypo says what is sold (goods → Article 2) or done (services, land, employment → common law) in its first sentence; about a quarter of Contracts hypos are UCC.
4. **No statistics** in `say`/`why` beyond NCBE's own fractions phrased as NCBE phrases them ("NCBE says about half of the Torts questions are on negligence"). Never "this is tested every year".
5. **No NCBE question text, no commercial-outline text.** Fact patterns are our own; the ~860 drafts in the research files are starting points to be rewritten with fresh facts and checked (the research says the best answer sits at (A) in some files: the unit file must set `answer` and let the engine shuffle). The three distinctive NCBE sample fact patterns named in `crim.md` §15.3 and `questions.md` are off limits.
6. **Stems the way the exam writes them:** no proper names, "State A / State B", 40-220 words, one call sentence, four parallel options, ≥ 60% in the "Yes/No, because" shape, "because/unless/if" as the hinge, the minimum facts that make the key right and the three distractors plausible. Each distractor must be wrong for a stateable reason that `why` states and `trap` classifies.
7. **Recent law:** anything decided after mid-2024 is `recent: true`, shipped as an extension card with the badge "New in 2025/26: probably not scored on your 2027 MBE", never as the only path to a skill. Old rules that an option could cite (Lemon, Humphrey's Executor, pre-Gant car searches, Roe) appear only as `trap` type 11.
8. **Currency flags:** `lawVersion: "fre-2026-12-01"` on any item whose answer or reason turns on FRE 801(d)(1)(A); write the key so that its reason is right under both texts where possible, and re-check on Dec 1, 2026 whether Congress acted.
9. Keep `say` ≤ 160 characters, warm and dry. Mascot lines never joke about failing.
10. Run `npm test` before handing the unit in. Zero errors; read the warnings; every `unsure` is listed in the unit's header comment with what a reviewer must check.

### u1 · Read the call: how an MBE question works

It uses only law that needs no prior lesson (the forum-defendant rule, FRE 406 habit, UCC 2-209 modification, pure comparative fault, Miranda custody + interrogation) so it can run on day one.

Quiz: tf "a federal counterclaim gives federal-question jurisdiction" style is NOT here; keep it to format facts and assumptions ("In a Torts question that says nothing, which fault rule applies?").

### u2 · Civil Procedure I: getting into federal court

Focus: NCBE outline categories I (jurisdiction and venue) and II (law applied by federal courts); with u3's motions these are the "two-thirds" categories. Assumption card: FRCP as currently in effect (Dec 1, 2025 amendments) + 28 U.S.C. §§1331, 1332, 1367, 1391, 1404, 1406, 1441-1447. Stems end "Does the court have subject-matter jurisdiction?", "Was the case properly removed?", "Should the court grant the motion to dismiss for lack of personal jurisdiction?", "Which law governs?".

Read: `civpro.md` §0-3 (briefing, both outlines, product implications, the numbers cheat-sheet), §4-8 (rules and ~45 fact patterns for cp1-cp5), §17 (recent cases, `recent`), §18 (trap catalog items 2-5, 8, 13-14), §19 (open items: every [K] is `general`, the unsettled doctrines (snap removal, supplemental jurisdiction over permissive counterclaims) are `unsure` or stem-resolved). `questions.md` §9 Civil Procedure. `hooks.jsonl` subject "Civil Procedure" (International Shoe, Pennoyer, Burnham "tag, you're it", Hertz, Mullane, Erie).

Lessons:
1. **Federal-question jurisdiction** (`rule` x2, `hypo` x4, `steps`): §1331 and the well-pleaded complaint rule (a federal defense or counterclaim does not count; declaratory-judgment mirror rule); embedded federal issues under *Grable* / *Gunn v. Minton* as an ordered four-factor `build` (necessarily raised, actually disputed, substantial, no disruption of the federal-state balance); exclusive federal jurisdiction (patent, copyright, bankruptcy) as a `sort`.
2. **Diversity** (`rule` x3, `hypo` x5, `num`): complete diversity (*Strawbridge*); citizenship of individuals (domicile: presence + intent to remain), corporations (incorporation + principal place of business = nerve center, *Hertz v. Friend*), LLCs and partnerships (every member), representatives (decedent's/infant's citizenship), aliens and permanent residents, time of filing; amount in controversy over $75,000 exclusive of interest and costs, aggregation rules (one plaintiff vs one defendant: all claims; multiple plaintiffs: no, except common undivided interest; counterclaims), legal-certainty test, injunction valuation; CAFA over $5,000,000 and minimal diversity as a `num`; the domestic-relations and probate exceptions as `general`.
3. **Supplemental jurisdiction** (`rule` x2, `hypo` x4, `steps`): §1367(a) same case or controversy (common nucleus, *Gibbs*); §1367(b) restricts only **plaintiffs'** claims against parties joined under Rules 14, 19, 20, 24 in diversity-only cases; *Exxon v. Allapattah* (one plaintiff meets the amount, others ride; but complete diversity still required); §1367(c) discretion to decline; §1367(d) 30-day tolling; the `steps` card: anchor claim → same nucleus → diversity-only? → plaintiff's claim against Rule 14/19/20/24 party? → discretion.
4. **Removal and remand** (`rule` x3, `hypo` x5, `num` x4, `steps`): §1441(a) only defendants, only to the embracing district; all properly joined and served defendants must join (§1446(b)(2)(A)); 30 days from service of the initial pleading, each defendant its own 30 days, later paper 30 days; the one-year limit for diversity removals unless bad faith (§1446(c)); the **forum-defendant rule** (§1441(b)(2), waivable, applies to diversity only, "properly joined and served"); federal-question removal of separate state claims (§1441(c) severance and remand); remand motion within 30 days for non-jurisdictional defects, SMJ any time (§1447(c)); remand orders unreviewable (§1447(d)) except §§1442/1443; *Home Depot v. Jackson* (counterclaim defendant cannot remove); *Royal Canin v. Wullschleger* (2025, drop all federal claims after removal → remand). Snap removal and the forum-defendant trap as `unsure`/stem-resolved.
5. **Personal jurisdiction** (`rule` x4, `hypo` x6, `build`): the long-arm + due-process two-step; traditional bases (presence/tag *Burnham*, domicile, consent, waiver); general jurisdiction "at home" (*Goodyear*, *Daimler*, *Ford*: incorporation + principal place of business; *Mallory* 2023 registration-consent where the statute says so, `recent`-ish, fractured); specific jurisdiction as a `build`: purposeful availment (*World-Wide Volkswagen*, *Burger King*, *Asahi* stream-of-commerce split stated as split) → arises out of or relates to (*Bristol-Myers Squibb*, *Walden v. Fiore*, *Ford* 2021 rejects strict causation) → fair play and substantial justice (five factors); internet contacts; Rule 4(k)(1)(A) borrows the state long-arm, 4(k)(1)(B) 100-mile bulge for Rule 14/19 parties, 4(k)(2) federal claims against defendants not subject to any state's courts; in rem and quasi in rem after *Shaffer v. Heitner*; *Fuld v. PLO* (2025) as `recent`.
6. **Service and notice; the Rule 12 waiver trap** (`rule` x3, `hypo` x4, `num` x4, `sort`): Rule 4: who may serve (nonparty 18+), methods for individuals (personal, abode with a person of suitable age and discretion who resides there, agent, state-law methods), corporations (officer or agent, or state law), 4(d) waiver (30 days to return, 60 to answer, no waiver of PJ or venue objections, costs for refusing), 4(m) 90 days with mandatory extension for good cause; constitutional notice (*Mullane*: reasonably calculated under all the circumstances; *Jones v. Flowers*); Rule 12(b)(2)-(5) waived if omitted from the first Rule 12 motion or answer, 12(b)(1) never, 12(b)(6)/(7) through trial, as a `sort` and a `cite` ("2-3-4-5" from `hooks.jsonl`).
7. **Venue, transfer, forum non conveniens** (`rule` x3, `hypo` x5, `build`): §1391(b)(1) any defendant resides **if all defendants reside in the same State**, (b)(2) substantial part of events or property, (b)(3) fallback; residence of individuals (domicile), entities (wherever subject to PJ for that action; for corporations in multi-district States, the district where contacts would suffice), aliens (any district); §1404(a) transfer for convenience to any district where it might have been brought or to which all parties consent, **transferor's law follows** (*Van Dusen*, *Ferens*) except forum-selection-clause transfers (*Atlantic Marine*: the clause controls, plaintiff's choice gets no weight, transferee's law); §1406(a) wrong venue: dismiss or transfer, transferee's law; §1631 lack of jurisdiction; forum non conveniens for foreign or state courts (*Piper Aircraft*: adequate alternative forum + private/public interest factors; a less favourable law is not enough).
8. **Erie, Klaxon, federal common law** (`rule` x3, `hypo` x5, `steps`): the *Hanna* two-track `steps` card: is there a federal rule/statute on point and valid (Rules Enabling Act, *Shady Grove*; *Berk v. Choy* 2026 `recent`) → apply it; otherwise *Erie*/*York* outcome-determinative test with the twin aims (forum shopping, inequitable administration, *Hanna* dicta; *Byrd* balancing); classic outcomes: statutes of limitations and tolling (*York*, *Walker*), burden of proof, standards for new trial on damages (*Gasperini*), choice of law (*Klaxon*: forum State's choice-of-law rules), §1404 transfers (*Van Dusen*); federal common law survives for interstate disputes, admiralty, federal proprietary interests (*Clearfield Trust*), implied rights (`exam: ["mbe"]`, NextGen omits it). The *Shady Grove* item must not ask the learner to pick between the plurality and the Stevens concurrence.

Question mix: `issue` openers ("A federal defense, a state claim: does the federal court have jurisdiction?"), `hypo` with `grid`, `transfer`, `build` (Grable factors, specific-jurisdiction test, §1391(b)), `steps` (supplemental, removal, Erie), `sort` (waived or not; at home or not), `num` (30 days, 1 year, $75,000, 90 days, 100 miles, 21 days), `pairs` (case ↔ holding), 1-2 `trap`. Roundup: a 10-question Civ Pro I timed set plus Cross-Examination numbers.

### u4 · Constitutional Law I: the structure

Focus: outline I (judicial review), II (separation of powers), III (nation and states): about half of the 25 questions. "Majority vs minority" barely exists here; the trap is a true rule from the wrong test (Tenth Amendment where the issue is preemption, Eleventh Amendment where the defendant is a county). Lessons:
1. **Homicide I: murder and malice** (`rule` x3, `hypo` x5, `build`): common-law murder = unlawful killing with malice aforethought; malice `build` (intent to kill, intent to inflict serious bodily harm, depraved heart (extreme recklessness, conscious disregard of a very high risk), felony murder); deadly-weapon inference; statutory degrees: first-degree premeditated and deliberate (planning, lying in wait, buying the weapon; how much time is a `split` the stem must say) and enumerated felonies; second-degree everything else with malice; MPC 210.2 (purposely/knowingly or recklessness manifesting extreme indifference, no degrees) as a `stance` card; "intent to scare" does not defeat depraved heart.
2. **Homicide II: manslaughter and felony murder** (`rule` x4, `hypo` x6, `build`): voluntary manslaughter `build` (adequate provocation (objective: serious battery, mutual combat, discovery of adultery; words alone not enough at common law, `split`), actual provocation, no reasonable cooling time, no actual cooling); imperfect self-defense (`split`, minority) ; MPC extreme mental or emotional disturbance with a reasonable explanation from the actor's viewpoint; involuntary manslaughter (criminal negligence / recklessness; misdemeanor-manslaughter: the unlawful act must cause the death, malum in se or dangerous); **felony murder** rules: inherently dangerous felony (BARRK list as a hook, flagged `general`), merger (assault and manslaughter cannot be the predicate), the killing during the felony or immediate flight until temporary safety, agency rule (majority: a killing by the victim or police is not felony murder; proximate-cause minority `split`), co-felon liability, *Enmund*/*Tison* death-penalty limits as a case card, eggshell victim (heart attack from fright counts), MPC: no felony-murder rule, only a rebuttable presumption of recklessness for listed felonies.
3. **Theft crimes and property crimes** (`rule` x5, `hypo` x6, `pairs`): larceny `build` (trespassory taking and carrying away of personal property of another with intent to permanently deprive; asportation slight; abandoned vs lost/mislaid; claim of right (honest, even unreasonable) defeats it; continuing-trespass doctrine; larceny by trick passes possession only), embezzlement (lawful possession then fraudulent conversion; intent to return money is no defense), false pretenses (false representation of a past or present fact, knowing, intent to defraud, **title passes**), the three as a `pairs`/`stance` ("Larceny takes, embezzlement keeps, false pretenses tricks you into giving"), receiving stolen property (knowing it is stolen at receipt; a sting on recovered goods = attempt, factual impossibility is no defense), robbery (larceny + from the person or presence + by force or intimidation of immediate harm; a future threat is extortion; pickpocket is not robbery), burglary at common law `build` (breaking (opening a closed, unlocked door counts; constructive breaking by fraud or threat; not through an open door), entering (any part of the body or an instrument), dwelling of another, at night, with intent to commit a felony at the time of entry) and the modern statutory stripping (any structure, any time, no breaking), arson (malicious burning of the dwelling of another; charring not scorching; negligence is not malice; modern statutes expand), modern theft consolidation.
4. **Crimes against the person; possession** (`rule` x4, `hypo` x5, `sort`): battery (unlawful application of force; general intent; consent and reasonable discipline defenses; aggravated by weapon or victim), assault (attempted battery (specific intent) or intentional creation of reasonable apprehension; conditional threats; words alone), mayhem; rape (common law: sexual intercourse without consent by force or threat; fraud in the factum vs fraud in the inducement (not rape); marital exemption abolished in modern law; mistake of fact as to consent `split`), statutory rape (strict liability as to age, majority; MPC reasonable-mistake defense above age ten), kidnapping (unlawful confinement + movement or concealment; movement merely incidental to another crime `split`), false imprisonment, possession offenses (knowing control long enough to terminate; constructive and joint possession; MPC 2.01(4)). `sort`: specific intent / general intent / malice / strict liability for a list of crimes (teach as "most courts" for assault, kidnapping, receiving).
5. **Attempt, solicitation, conspiracy** (`rule` x4, `hypo` x6, `stance`, `sort`): attempt (specific intent to commit the target crime + an act beyond mere preparation: dangerous-proximity (common law) vs **substantial step** (MPC, broader); no attempt of reckless/negligent crimes; factual impossibility no defense, true legal impossibility a defense; abandonment: none at common law, MPC voluntary and complete renunciation; attempt merges), solicitation (asking another to commit a crime with intent that it be committed; complete on the asking; merges into the completed crime or conspiracy; MPC renunciation), conspiracy (agreement + specific intent to agree and to achieve the objective; **bilateral at common law** (two guilty minds; a feigned accomplice or undercover officer cannot conspire) vs **unilateral under the MPC**; overt act required in most modern statutes, none at common law; *Pinkerton* liability for foreseeable crimes in furtherance; Wharton's Rule; withdrawal ends liability for later crimes but is not a defense to the conspiracy itself; MPC withdrawal with thwarting; **conspiracy does not merge**); `sort`: merges or not; `stance`: CL vs MPC on bilateral, abandonment, substantial step.
6. **Parties, acts, mental states, mistake, causation** (`rule` x5, `hypo` x6, `build`): accomplice liability (aid, encourage or counsel with intent that the crime be committed, plus intent to assist; mere presence and knowledge are not enough; words of encouragement are; natural and probable consequences (`split`); principals and accessories, accessory after the fact is a separate lesser offense; withdrawal must neutralise the assistance; the person protected by the statute cannot be an accomplice); voluntary act (reflex, seizure, sleepwalking not voluntary; prior voluntary act (driving knowing of seizures) suffices; status offenses *Robinson*; omissions need a legal duty: statute, relationship, contract, voluntary assumption + seclusion, creation of the peril); mental states (common law: specific intent crimes list, malice, general intent, strict liability; MPC purposely/knowingly/recklessly/negligently `build`, recklessness default, transferred intent, concurrence); mistake of fact (negates specific intent if honest; general intent only if reasonable; never strict liability) and mistake of law (no defense, even on counsel's advice, except reliance on an official statement (MPC 2.04(3)), knowledge of the law as an element (*Cheek*), *Lambert* notice); causation (but-for + proximate; dependent intervening acts (medical negligence, victim's flight) do not break the chain unless grossly abnormal; independent unforeseeable coincidences do (the ambulance crash); take the victim as found; hastening death counts; *Burrage* for "results from").
7. **Responsibility, justification, excuse, jurisdiction** (`rule` x6, `hypo` x7, `stance`, `sort`): insanity tests `pairs` (M'Naghten: disease of the mind → did not know the nature and quality or that it was wrong (majority); irresistible impulse; Durham product (NH only, `unsure`); MPC substantial capacity to appreciate wrongfulness or conform conduct); burden may be on the defendant (*Leland*; federal: clear and convincing); *Kahler* (2020) states may confine mental illness to a mens rea defense; competency is a separate question (*Dusky*); diminished capacity; intoxication (voluntary: only negates specific intent, never general intent, malice or recklessness (MPC 2.08(2)); involuntary: like insanity); self-defense (reasonable belief of imminent unlawful force, proportionate; deadly force only against deadly force or serious bodily harm; retreat: no duty (majority) vs retreat if completely safe except at home (minority, castle doctrine); initial aggressor loses it unless withdrawal communicated or the victim escalates; imperfect self-defense `split`; MPC subjective belief with recklessness liability), defense of others (reasonable-belief majority vs alter-ego minority), defense of property (never deadly force; *Katko*), arrest and resisting (no right to resist a lawful arrest; may resist excessive force; *Garner*/*Graham*/*Barnes v. Felix* 2025 cross-reference), necessity (greater harm avoided, no fault, not for homicide (*Dudley & Stephens*)), duress (imminent death or serious bodily harm threat to self or another, reasonable, no escape; **not a defense to murder** at common law; MPC person of reasonable firmness, murder not excluded), entrapment (subjective: inducement + no predisposition, federal and majority; objective MPC minority), consent (only where it negates an element: rape, some batteries; not for serious harm), jurisdiction (conduct or result in the state; dual sovereignty *Heath*, *Gamble*). `sort`: defense available or not for murder (duress no, necessity no, self-defense yes, insanity yes).

Question mix: `issue` ("Larceny, embezzlement or false pretenses?"), `hypo` with the "No crime" option in `crime` calls, `transfer`, `build` (malice, voluntary manslaughter, larceny, burglary, MPC mental states), `stance` (CL vs MPC on eight points), `sort` (merger; specific vs general intent; defenses to murder), `pairs` (crime ↔ defining element; insanity test ↔ wording), `argue` (best defense), `trap` x2 (anger ≠ no premeditation; intent to return ≠ no embezzlement). Question mix: `issue` ("Assumed or subject to?"), `hypo`/`grid` with `title`, `liable`, `remedy` calls, `transfer`, `build` (waterfall, adverse possession, six covenants), `steps` (recording-act priority), `sort` (marketable or not; void or voidable; present or future covenant; BFP or not), `stance` (lien vs title theory; buyer vs seller risk of loss), `num` (10% deposit, 3-year lease exception, statutory periods as stem-given), `pairs` (transfer type ↔ liability; statute ↔ winner), `trap` x3 (clogging; deed in lieu and juniors; estoppel by deed with a quitclaim). Roundup: 15 timed hypos (5 per category III-V) plus a Cross-Examination recording-act run.

### u12 · Torts

Focus: all four outline categories; negligence is about half (lessons 2-5 and half the drill), intentional torts, strict/products liability and other torts share the rest. Assumption card (verbatim NCBE): survival actions and wrongful death are available; **joint and several liability and pure comparative fault** apply unless the question says otherwise. Stems: "Will the plaintiff prevail?", "Is the defendant liable?", "What is the plaintiff's strongest argument?", "The defendant's best defense is", "How much will the plaintiff recover?".

Read: `torts.md` §0 (outline, NextGen diffs: animals, last clear chance, interference, wrongful death are `exam: ["mbe"]`), **§1 (defaults and one-liners: drill until automatic)**, §2 (intentional torts + INT items), §3-4 (negligence A and B + NEG/CAU/MUL/VIC/DEF/DAM items), §5 (strict, products, nuisance + STR/PRO/NUI), §6 (defamation and the SCOTUS cases read: Sullivan, Gertz, Dun & Bradstreet, Hepps, Milkovich, Hustler, Time v. Hill, Cox, Florida Star; privacy; misrepresentation; interference + DFM/PRI/MIS items), §7 (twelve traps, distractor patterns, prompts, unit plan, the eight items to hold back: INT-3, STR-6, DFM-5, DEF-4, CAU-7, VIC-3, MIS-2, PRO-4 → `unsure`), §8. The best answer is always (A) in that file: the unit sets `answer` and the engine shuffles. `hooks.jsonl`: Palsgraf, Vosburg, Garratt v. Dailey (Ruth Garratt; the boy moved the chair), Summers v. Tice, Carroll Towing (B<PL), Tarasoff, Katko (facts verified), MacPherson (wheel maker unnamed), Seinfeld's Good Samaritan myth, Home Alone, Wile E. Coyote v. Acme, Pennzoil (medium confidence on figures).

Lessons:
1. **Intentional torts and their defenses** (`rule` x5, `hypo` x6, `build` x3, `sort`): intent (purpose or substantial certainty; motive and mistake irrelevant; children and the mentally ill can intend; transferred intent among assault, battery, false imprisonment, trespass to land, trespass to chattels), battery `build` (intentional, harmful or offensive contact, with the person or something closely connected), assault `build` (intentional, reasonable apprehension of an imminent contact; apprehension not fear; words alone usually insufficient unless with conduct), false imprisonment (confinement in a bounded area, no reasonable means of escape known to the plaintiff, awareness or harm; shopkeeper's privilege: reasonable grounds, manner, time), IIED (extreme and outrageous, intent or recklessness, severe distress (no physical injury needed); bystanders: present, close relative or bodily harm; common carriers and public figures (*Hustler*, *Snyder* `general`)), trespass to land (intentional entry; mistake no defense; no damage needed), trespass to chattels (intermeddling, actual damage or dispossession) vs conversion (serious interference → forced sale at full value), defenses `sort` (consent (express, implied, scope, capacity; medical emergency consent), self-defense and defense of others (reasonable belief, proportionate, retreat not required (majority), no deadly force for property), defense of property (reasonable force, request to leave first, no spring guns *Katko*), recapture of chattels (fresh pursuit), **private necessity** (entry privileged but pay for actual damage, *Vincent v. Lake Erie*) vs **public necessity** (no liability), arrest privileges, parental discipline).
2. **Negligence I: duty and the standard of care** (`rule` x5, `hypo` x6, `sort`): the five elements `build` (duty, breach, actual cause, proximate cause, damages); duty to foreseeable plaintiffs (*Palsgraf* majority (Cardozo) vs Andrews minority as `stance`); rescuers are foreseeable (*Wagner*); **no duty to act** with the exceptions (special relationship: common carrier, innkeeper, school, custodian, employer; defendant created the peril, even innocently; voluntary undertaking that increases the risk or induces reliance (Rest. 3d §42, NCBE-cited); statutory duties; *Tarasoff* therapist duty to warn `general`; Good Samaritan statutes limit liability of helpers, they do not create a duty); duty to control third parties (negligent entrustment, employer, parents for known dangerous propensities); the reasonably prudent person (objective; physical disabilities counted, mental illness and low intelligence not; superior knowledge used; emergency doctrine; children: like age, intelligence, experience, **except adult activities** (snowmobile, NCBE's example); professionals: the profession's standard, specialists nationally; informed consent `split` (patient-centred vs physician-centred)); custom is evidence, not conclusive (*T.J. Hooper*); *Carroll Towing* B<PL as a hook; **negligence per se** (statute's class of persons + type of harm; violation = breach (majority) or evidence; excuses (compliance more dangerous, incapacity, emergency); compliance is not conclusive; licensing statutes usually do not qualify); **res ipsa loquitur** `build` (the event ordinarily does not occur without negligence, the instrumentality in the defendant's exclusive control (modern: the defendant more likely than not responsible), plaintiff did not contribute; effect: inference, gets to the jury; *Ybarra* multiple defendants `general`).
3. **Negligence II: owners and occupiers; NIED; economic loss** (`rule` x4, `hypo` x6, `pairs`, `stance`): the traditional entrant scheme `pairs` (undiscovered trespasser: no duty; discovered/anticipated trespasser: warn of known artificial dangerous conditions, refrain from wilful conduct; child trespasser: Rest. 2d §339 five factors `build` (owner knows children likely to trespass, knows the condition poses unreasonable risk of serious harm, children cannot appreciate the risk, utility and burden slight compared with the risk, failure to exercise reasonable care); licensee (social guests): warn of known concealed dangers, no inspection; invitee (business visitors, public invitees): reasonable care including **inspection**; exceeding the scope changes status; lessors, vendors, off-premises (natural conditions no duty, artificial yes); the modern reasonable-care-to-all approach (*Rowland*) as a `stance` (NCBE's MEE analysis: most states keep the trespasser category, so the traditional scheme is the MBE-safe default); attractive nuisance is §339; firefighter's rule `general`; **NIED** (zone of danger (*Gottshall*) with physical manifestation `split`; bystander: close relative + contemporaneous perception + presence (*Thing*/*Dillon* as the modern majority); direct duty cases: mishandled corpse, false death notice (no impact needed)); **pure economic loss**: no recovery in negligence without personal injury or property damage (*Robins Dry Dock*; accountants' limited-group rule under §552 belongs to lesson 8; *East River* for products).
4. **Negligence III: causation** (`rule` x4, `hypo` x6, `sort`): actual cause (but-for; **substantial factor** for multiple sufficient causes; **alternative liability** (*Summers v. Tice*: burden shifts to the two negligent defendants); market-share liability (*Sindell*, minority, `split`); loss of chance `split`; concert of action); proximate cause as scope of the risk / foreseeability (the kind of harm must be foreseeable, not the manner or extent); **eggshell plaintiff** (take the plaintiff as found: extent of harm); intervening causes `sort` (foreseeable intervening acts do not supersede: medical malpractice, rescue attempts, subsequent accidents while weakened, negligence of others, crimes that are the very risk created; **unforeseeable, extraordinary** intervening acts supersede: acts of God, unforeseeable intentional torts or crimes (`split`, CAU-7 `unsure`), highly extraordinary results); *Wagon Mound* vs *Polemis* as a `stance` (foreseeability is the exam rule); *Palsgraf* placement (duty or proximate cause: either way the answer is "unforeseeable, no liability").
5. **Negligence IV: multiple tortfeasors, vicarious liability, defenses, damages** (`rule` x6, `hypo` x7, `pairs`, `stance`): **joint and several liability** (the default: each liable for the whole indivisible harm; contribution by comparative share (modern) or pro rata; **indemnity** for vicarious liability, retailers against manufacturers, and the "passive vs active" minority; several-only liability only if the stem says); **release** of one tortfeasor (modern: does not release others; credit for the amount paid); satisfaction; vicarious liability `pairs` (employer for employee's torts within the scope (control test; frolic vs detour; intentional torts only if the job involves force or furthers the business, VIC-3 `unsure`); not for independent contractors except nondelegable duties (inherently dangerous activities, duties to the public, invitees, statutory duties) and apparent agency; negligent hiring/supervision is direct liability; parents not vicariously liable at common law (statutes, negligent supervision); car owners not liable except family-purpose and owner-consent statutes, negligent entrustment; partners and joint venturers; the business-owner/customer and bailor/bailee lines); **defenses** `stance` (pure comparative fault = the default, reduces recovery never bars; modified 50%/51% and contributory negligence + last clear chance only when the stem says (AL, DC, MD, NC, VA named by NCBE); express assumption of risk (releases; not for gross negligence or against public policy); implied **primary** assumption of risk = no duty (sports participants, spectators (DEF-4 `unsure`)); **secondary** merges into comparative fault; avoidable consequences and mitigation); damages (personal injury: medical, lost earnings, pain and suffering; property: cost to repair or diminution; **collateral source rule** (traditional: no reduction; modified by statute in some states `split`); punitive only for wilful/wanton conduct, *Gore* guideposts and *State Farm* single-digit ratio; nominal not available in negligence; **survival** (decedent's own claim continues) vs **wrongful death** (survivors' pecuniary loss, consortium; defenses against the decedent apply; `exam: ["mbe"]`); loss of consortium derivative); the American rule on fees (`general`).
6. **Strict liability: animals, abnormally dangerous activities; nuisance** (`rule` x4, `hypo` x5, `sort`): wild animals: strict liability for harm from their dangerous propensities (not to trespassers, except vicious watchdogs `general`); domestic animals: only with **knowledge of the dangerous propensity** (one-bite is a hook, not a rule; prior bite not required; dog-bite statutes `split`); trespassing livestock strict (`exam: ["mbe"]`); abnormally dangerous activities `build` (Rest. 2d §520 six factors: high risk, serious harm, cannot be eliminated by reasonable care, not common usage, inappropriate to the place, value outweighed by danger; Rest. 3d §20 two-part as `stance`): blasting, crop dusting, fumigation, storing explosives, hazardous waste; **the harm must result from the abnormal risk** (STR-6 `unsure` for scope); defenses: comparative fault reduces (modern), assumption of risk; **private nuisance** (substantial and unreasonable interference with use and enjoyment; balancing utility against gravity (*Boomer* damages instead of injunction); sensitive plaintiff; "coming to the nuisance" is a factor, not a bar (*Spur v. Del Webb*); remedies: damages, injunction, abatement by self-help with notice); **public nuisance** (unreasonable interference with a public right; a private plaintiff needs **special injury different in kind**); trespass vs nuisance `sort` (physical invasion vs interference; intangible intrusions).
7. **Products liability** (`rule` x5, `hypo` x6, `build`, `sort`): the five theories `pairs` (intent, negligence, strict products liability, implied warranties (2-314/2-315, privity under 2-318 A/B/C by stem), express warranty and misrepresentation); **strict products liability** `build` (commercial seller in the business (manufacturer, distributor, retailer; not casual sellers, services, used-goods dealers `split`, lessors yes), product defective when it left the seller's hands (manufacturing: departs from design, strict in the true sense; design: reasonable alternative design under Rest. 3d §2 vs consumer expectations `stance`, PRO-4 `unsure`; **warning**: foreseeable risks not obvious, learned-intermediary doctrine for prescription drugs, post-sale duties `general`), caused the plaintiff's injury, foreseeable use including **foreseeable misuse** (not a defense), physical harm (not pure economic loss to the product itself, *East River*)); who may sue (any foreseeable plaintiff: users, bystanders; no privity); who may be sued (every commercial seller in the chain; indemnity up the chain; successor liability `general`); defenses `sort` (comparative fault reduces (majority), assumption of risk (knowing use of a defective product), substantial alteration after sale, unforeseeable misuse, state-of-the-art for design and warning, compliance with regulation is evidence not a bar, preemption cross-reference to u4 (*Wyeth*/*Mensing*), disclaimers ineffective for personal injury under strict liability (2-719(3) cross-reference)).
8. **Defamation, privacy, misrepresentation, interference** (`rule` x6, `hypo` x7, `build`, `pairs`): defamation common-law `build` (defamatory statement of fact about the plaintiff (of and concerning; groups; the dead cannot be defamed), publication to a third person (negligent or intentional; republishers liable; §230 immunity for platforms `general`), falsity (constitutional burden on the plaintiff for public concern, *Hepps*), fault, damages: libel (presumed general damages) vs slander (special damages required unless slander per se: crime of moral turpitude, loathsome disease, business/profession, serious sexual misconduct)); constitutional overlay `pairs` (public official/figure (all-purpose or limited-purpose): **actual malice** = knowledge of falsity or reckless disregard, clear and convincing (*Sullivan*, *Gertz*, *St. Amant* failure to investigate is not enough); private figure + public concern: at least negligence, actual injury, presumed/punitive only with actual malice (*Gertz*); private figure + private concern: presumed and punitive allowed (*Dun & Bradstreet*); no separate opinion privilege: "provable as false" (*Milkovich*); parody (*Hustler*)); defenses (truth, absolute privileges (judicial, legislative, executive, spousal), qualified privileges (common interest, fair report, protecting one's own interest) lost by malice or excessive publication, consent); **privacy** `pairs` (intrusion upon seclusion: offensive intrusion, no publication needed; appropriation of name or likeness for commercial advantage; public disclosure of private facts: highly offensive, not of legitimate public concern, truth no defense (*Cox*, *Florida Star* public records), newsworthiness; false light: highly offensive, publicity, actual malice for public interest (*Time v. Hill*); defenses: consent, privileges; the right is personal, dies with the plaintiff `general`); **misrepresentation** (fraud `build`: false representation of material fact, scienter (knowledge or reckless disregard), intent to induce reliance, justifiable reliance (not on puffery or opinion unless expertise/fiduciary), damages; nondisclosure only with a duty; **negligent misrepresentation** in the business/professional context to a limited group whose reliance was intended (§552, MIS-2 `unsure`)); **interference** (`exam: ["mbe"]`): with contract (valid contract, knowledge, intentional inducement of breach, damages; competitor's privilege covers prospective relations and at-will contracts, not inducing breach of a definite-term contract), with prospective economic advantage, injurious falsehood; wrongful civil proceedings and abuse of process in one card (`general`).

Question mix: `issue` ("Negligence, strict liability, or neither?"), `hypo`/`grid` with `prevail` and `liable` calls, `transfer`, `build` (negligence elements, battery, §339, res ipsa, strict products, fraud, defamation), `sort` (superseding or not; within scope of employment; strictly liable; trespass or nuisance; privileged; actual malice required), `stance` (pure comparative vs contributory; Palsgraf majority vs Andrews; traditional vs Rowland; consumer expectations vs RAD), `pairs` (case ↔ rule; entrant ↔ duty; plaintiff type ↔ fault standard; privacy tort ↔ element), `argue` (strongest argument; best defense), `trap` x3 (contributory negligence as the default; "no cause of action because the victim died"; adding physical injury to IIED). Roundup: 25-question Torts subject set (12-13 negligence) in 45 minutes, plus a Cross-Examination "pure comparative or contributory?" run.
